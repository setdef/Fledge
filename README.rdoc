== Ship a Ruby App to People Who Don't Have Ruby

Fledge[https://github.com/setdef/Fledge] turns a Ruby program you already
run into an app that people without Ruby can download and run. Your users
unpack one file and run it. They install nothing, and never learn what Ruby
is.

Fledge runs on Ruby 4.1, with Bundler: on its preview, 4.1.0-preview1,
until Ruby 4.1.0 is released. Add it to your project's +Gemfile+:

  gem "fledge", git: "https://github.com/setdef/Fledge", tag: "v0.16.0", group: :development

=== A First App

A folder named +greeter+ holds a one-line +main.rb+ and a +Gemfile+ with
Fledge in it:

  $ bundle exec fledge build
  dist/Greeter-arm64-darwin.tar.gz
  $ tar -xzf dist/Greeter-arm64-darwin.tar.gz -C /tmp
  $ /tmp/Greeter-arm64-darwin/Greeter
  Hello, Ruby!

The archive is 29 MB. Anyone with an Apple silicon Mac on macOS 14 or newer
can run it, with no Ruby on their computer.

That archive holds Fledge too, about 3 MB of it, as it holds your other
development gems: Bundler installs a gem from git as its whole repository.
Bundler's +without+ setting cannot leave Fledge out, because
<tt>without development</tt> leaves it out of your own bundle as well. To
build apps without it, put the line above in a +Gemfile+ of its own, in a
folder outside your project, run <tt>bundle install</tt> there, and from
there build your project by its path:
<tt>bundle exec fledge build ~/code/greeter</tt>.

=== What You Get

Fledge copies your program, your project, and the gems your
+Gemfile.lock+ locks into a Ruby that runs from any folder, and adds a
launcher that starts your program as <tt>bundle exec</tt> would. Your code
runs as it does under <tt>ruby main.rb</tt>, whether or not your users
installed a Ruby, with a few
{differences}[https://github.com/setdef/Fledge/blob/trunk/doc/differences_from_ruby.md]
your code can see, each with its reason. The app carries the gems of every
group <tt>bundle exec</tt> loads, +development+ and +test+ included, less
the groups Bundler's +without+ setting names.

[Terminal apps]
  A <tt>.tar.gz</tt> on macOS and Linux, a <tt>.zip</tt> on Windows, with a
  SHA-256 beside each for Scoop, Homebrew, winget, Chocolatey, and the
  AUR.
[Windowed apps]
  A <tt>.app</tt> and a <tt>.dmg</tt> on macOS; a <tt>.zip</tt> and a setup
  program on Windows, and an MSIX package for the Microsoft Store if you
  ask; and a <tt>.tar.gz</tt> with a desktop entry and an AppImage on
  Linux, and a <tt>.deb</tt> if you ask. Each carries your name, icon,
  version, and the files your app opens.
[Phones]
  An Xcode or Gradle project for iPhone, iPad, and Android, with your
  gems' C, C++, and Rust extensions compiled for the phone, and the +ffi+
  gem and Ruby's +fiddle+ working there.
[Signing]
  <tt>--sign</tt> signs every program, library, bundle, and installer
  with your macOS identity or your Windows certificate, and
  <tt>--notarize</tt> has Apple notarize a Mac app.
[Frameworks]
  <tt>fledge build --from</tt> makes a new app from one Fledge already
  built, with no Bundler, network, or compiler on the computer.

Fledge builds for macOS 14 and newer on Apple silicon; Linux with glibc
2.39 or musl 1.2 and newer, on x86-64 and ARM64; Windows 10 and 11 on x64,
and Windows 11 on ARM64; iOS 15 and newer; and Android 8.0 and newer. Build
each platform's app on a computer of that platform, such as a CI runner of
each. On every change, CI runs each desktop app on a clean computer with
no Ruby (macOS 15, Windows Server 2025, Windows 11 on ARM64, Ubuntu 24.04,
Debian 13, and Alpine 3.24), and each phone app in the iOS 26 Simulator
and on Android 16 emulators. No CI job runs an app on macOS 14, Windows
10, iOS 15, or Android 8.0: Fledge builds each app for them, but no run
there proves it.

Fledge is 0.x, so any new version can break your build: read the changelog
before you update. CI signs apps only with a self-signed certificate, so
signing with a certificate from Apple or a certificate authority, and
notarizing with Apple, are untested. No Fledge app has been through a
store's review.
