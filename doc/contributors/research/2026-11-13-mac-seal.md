# How macOS treats a downloaded app that is sealed, edited, or unsealed

> Measured on 2026-11-13 by the Lota agent (Lota's orchestrating session) on the maintainer's Mac, macOS 27 on Apple silicon, at Fledge's request. Recorded here by Fledge's orchestrating agent. 

## The question

A Mac app Fledge built without `--sign` was sealed ad hoc: `codesign --force --sign - --identifier IDENTIFIER NAME.app` records a hash of every file in the bundle. A framework that fuses a game into its player app by hand, as LÖVE's fused games are made, copies a file into `Contents/Resources` and edits `Info.plist`, which breaks such a seal. What does a person who downloads each form see?

## The forms

A tiny C program in a minimal `.app`, built with clang, whose executable carries the linker's ad hoc signature:

- **A**, sealed ad hoc: `codesign --force --sign - --identifier`, Fledge's form.
- **B**, as A, then a file added to `Contents/Resources` and `CFBundleName` changed.
- **C**, the linker's signature alone, then the same edits.

LÖVE 12 as its project builds it: love2d/love `main` at def245102c, GitHub Actions run 41957924703, artifact `love-macos.zip`, SHA-256 `7494659f2540d62690de0537046dfa9063cae86f00bc4bb30c5a94cf26172407`. Its bundle carries no signature; its executable carries the linker's ad hoc one.

- **L1**, `love.app` as shipped.
- **L2**, the same with `game.love` added to `Contents/Resources` and `CFBundleName` changed.

Fledge's `examples/windowed_app`, built without `--sign` from commit 674d885a, which left an unsigned bundle unsealed (never landed): a thin arm64 launcher signed ad hoc, no `_CodeSignature`, zip SHA-256 `62638a3aec80c7709660233b04050e974f2c323d7cc7ad64172dcff4e9fd2e6c`.

- **F1**, as built.
- **F2**, with `game.lota` added to `Contents/Resources` and `CFBundleName` changed.

Each was quarantined as a download marks it, then opened.

## Results

| Form | `codesign --verify --deep --strict` | `spctl --assess` | Launched from a local copy | Opened after quarantine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | valid | rejected | ran | "Not Opened" |
| B | "invalid Info.plist (modified)" | rejected | ran | "is damaged and can't be opened" |
| C | "code has no resources but signature indicates they must be present" | rejected | ran | blocked (by launch order, likely "damaged"; its dialog was dismissed unread) |
| L1 | "code object is not signed at all" | "rejected, source=no usable signature" | | "Not Opened" |
| L2 | "code object is not signed at all" | "rejected, source=no usable signature" | | "Not Opened" (confirmed by screenshot) |
| F1 | "code has no resources but signature indicates they must be present" | the same | | "is damaged and can't be opened" (screenshot) |
| F2 | "code has no resources but signature indicates they must be present" | the same | | "is damaged and can't be opened" (screenshot) |

The two dialogs:

- "'NAME' Not Opened. Apple could not verify 'NAME' is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy." [Move to Trash] [Done]. System Settings, Privacy & Security, then offers "Open Anyway" for the app just refused.
- "'NAME' is damaged and can't be opened. You should move it to the Trash." [Cancel] [Move to Trash]. Nothing a person can do in System Settings opens it.

`syspolicyd` logged "Terminating process due to Gatekeeper rejection" for every quarantined launch of A, B, and C, and "Prompt shown (8, 0)" for L1 and L2.

## What it means

An unsealed bundle whose program carries an ad hoc signature, as every program that runs on Apple silicon must, is "damaged" to Gatekeeper, edited or not (C, F1, F2). Only a seal (A) or a program codesign calls "not signed at all" (L1, L2) gets the ordinary "Not Opened". LÖVE's `love.app` reads as not signed at all because its executable is universal and its x86_64 slice carries no signature (`codesign --verify --arch x86_64`); its arm64 slice alone gives C's message. A thin arm64 program cannot read that way and still run.

So Fledge keeps sealing an app it builds without `--sign` (ADR 050): leaving it unsealed would make every downloaded unsigned Fledge app "damaged". An app that is edited after the build is sealed again with `codesign --force --sign - --identifier IDENTIFIER NAME.app`, or rebuilt with `fledge build --from` (ADR 032), which does so. A launcher made universal with an unsigned x86_64 slice, as LÖVE's is, would let an edited app stay at "Not Opened"; it rests on how Gatekeeper treats a slice it cannot check, which nothing of Apple's documents, and was not taken.
