# Plan 003: The Rest of the Way to 1.0

## Status

Planned (requested). Agents wrote this plan and may reorder or rewrite it (AGENTS.md, Questions). Plan 002 is done: every target it planned builds on its own system and delivers on a fresh computer, and what it left unproven is gathered here, from the "Not yet" lines of its stories and of ADRs 005, 006, 008 to 012, 015, 016, and 018, and from `doc/contributors/questions.md`. Each story names the maintainer's decision it serves (`doc/contributors/decisions.md`), why it matters, and when it is done.

A story marked **1.0** must be done before 1.0; one marked **maybe 1.0** is decided when 1.0 is near; one marked **0.2.0** is the maintainer's priority for that release (decisions, 2026-11-02); one marked **after 1.0** is recorded so it is not lost. Three stories wait on money (accounts, certificates, and a way to run Android's arm64 build) and say so; work proceeds around them. The maintainer ruled on 2026-11-02 that 1.0 waits until at least some of those proofs exist, and that releases move from `0.0.1.pre.N` to ordinary 0.MINOR.PATCH versions, beginning with 0.1.0 (decisions, 2026-11-02).

## Stories

### A framework wrapper ships an app through Fledge **1.0**

**Why.** Lota (2D games), Tokra (desktop apps), and Rooibos (terminal UIs) are meant to call Fledge, and Fledge's design is for the developers who use them. Wrappers depend on Fledge; Fledge depends on no wrapper's repository (decisions, 2026-11-02). So the story is Fledge's side: that an app built on a wrapper's gems ships through `fledge build`, and that what a wrapper may rely on is written down.

**What.** Pick the wrapper whose app is closest to shipping, pin Fledge from a release tag in its Gemfile, and build and deliver one of its examples on each platform it supports with that release's runtimes. Fix what breaks in Fledge, and record in an ADR what a wrapper may rely on: the command, `fledge.yml`, exit statuses, and output.

**Done when.** An app built on a wrapper, with a tagged Fledge, runs on a fresh runner of each platform the wrapper supports, with no Ruby there, and an ADR states what a wrapper may rely on.

**Status.** Done on Fledge's side (requested), ADR 019. Rooibos is the wrapper closest to shipping (published, pure Ruby on a precompiled Rust gem, examples that need only a terminal; Tokra needs a webview and a display, and Lota is unpublished with no examples). Its website's first app, built from a project that pins Fledge `v0.0.1.pre.16` by tag with the release's runtimes, runs in a terminal on a fresh runner with no Ruby on `arm64-darwin`, `x86_64-linux`, `aarch64-linux`, and `x64-mingw-ucrt` (the `experiment/wrapper` branch, run 41360640888): the first screen, a typed key, Ctrl-C, and exit status 0. Building Rooibos's repository found a bug in Fledge, fixed: a locked gem with a RubyGems plugin whose loader the runtime has (`rdoc`, which `irb` asks for) failed the build; first released in `v0.0.1.pre.17`. ADR 019 records what a wrapper may rely on. Whether a wrapper's own repository adds CI of this shape, or builds Tokra's and Lota's apps, is that wrapper's to decide; Fledge waits on none of it. The unsent drafts in `doc/contributors/drafts/` tell Rooibos and Tokra, optionally, how to depend on a tag.

### iOS on a real device, and App Review **1.0, waits on money**

**Why.** The maintainer asked for iOS and iPadOS before 1.0 (decisions, 2026-10-27) and for an Apple Developer account at the last responsible moment (2026-10-28). The Simulator is not the device: Apple's sandbox on an iPhone differs (ADR 011, section 6), and App Review judges an embedded interpreter by guidelines 2.5.2 and 4.2.6.

**What.** When the maintainer opens the account (the maintainer ruled on 2026-11-02 that no money is spent now; decisions, 2026-11-02): sign `examples/hello` and `examples/mobile_app` with a development team, run them on a real iPhone and iPad (a device cloud for CI), and submit one to TestFlight.

**Done when.** A Fledge-built app runs on a real iPhone and iPad, and App Review accepts one into TestFlight.

**Status.** Partly done: a game built with `fledge build ios` ran on a real iPad on 2026-11-05, signed with a free Personal Team (ADR 025, section 6), so running on a device waits on no money; an iPhone run needs none either. What waits on money is TestFlight and App Review.

### Notarization and real trust chains **1.0, waits on money**

**Why.** Decision 17 asks that developers who have done Apple's and Microsoft's ceremony be able to sign so that neither system blocks their app. `fledge build --sign` signs with a developer's identity (ADR 016), but only a self-signed certificate has proven it.

**What.** When the maintainer opens the accounts (no money is spent now; decisions, 2026-11-02): `--notarize`, with notarytool and an App Store Connect API key, then stapler on the disk image; Azure Trusted Signing's signtool options; and Delivery jobs that check Gatekeeper's verdict (`spctl --assess`) and SmartScreen's on a downloaded copy. Find out whether YJIT still needs `com.apple.security.cs.allow-unsigned-executable-memory` under a Developer ID.

**Done when.** A notarized disk image opens on a fresh Mac with no warning, and a setup program signed through a real chain installs on a fresh Windows runner with no SmartScreen block.

### Android on arm64 **1.0, waits on money**

**Why.** Almost every Android phone is arm64-v8a, and CI runs that library on nothing: GitHub's runners emulate only x86-64 Android (ADR 011 and ADR 012). One manual run on an Apple-silicon Mac is not a gate.

**What.** Run the Mobile apps checks on an arm64-v8a emulator in CI, on an ARM64 runner with hardware virtualization if one becomes available, or by any other free means; no money is spent on it (decisions, 2026-11-02).

**Done when.** Every push runs the parity probe and the HTTPS script on arm64-v8a.

**Status.** Waiting on a free way to run it (requested): the maintainer ruled on 2026-11-02 that money is not spent on this now (decisions, 2026-11-02), so no device cloud, paid runner, or machine. Tried 2026-11-02 on `experiment/android-arm64`: no free GitHub-hosted runner (`ubuntu-24.04-arm`, `macos-14`, `macos-15`, `macos-26`, `windows-11-arm`) boots an arm64-v8a emulator with hardware acceleration, and Google publishes no emulator for Linux ARM64 or Windows ARM64 hosts (ADR 011, Consequences). Work proceeds on the default: the arm64-v8a library is built and its APK checked in CI, and the checks run on x86_64 emulators. Recheck when GitHub announces nested virtualization on an ARM64 runner.

### The gems real apps lock **1.0**

**Why.** Decision 20 and ADR 005 promise that an app's code runs as under `bundle exec`. Some Gemfiles still cannot ship: path gems outside the project, gem servers that need credentials or mirrors (`BUNDLE_MIRROR__*`), git submodules, path gems with extensions, optional groups (which ship like any other), and gem executables in `bin/` (ADR 005 and ADR 006).

**What.** Take each in turn, Bundler's own behavior as the reference: read its settings and credentials as Bundler reads them, check out submodules, and compile path gems as other gems are compiled. Refuse what still cannot work with a message that says why.

**Done when.** An example that uses each of these builds and delivers on every desktop platform, or Fledge refuses it with a message that names the gem and what to do.

**Status.** Done for the desktop platforms CI builds native gems on (requested), ADR 020. `examples/real_gems` uses every kind in one app and builds and delivers on `arm64-darwin`, `x86_64-linux`, `aarch64-linux`, and `x64-mingw-ucrt`: a gem through a loopback mirror that wants a login (Bundler's own mirror and credential settings), a path gem from a folder outside the project, a path gem with a C extension, a git gem with a submodule, an optional group the project's `with` asks for, and a gem's executable run with no Ruby. Bundler's own behavior is the reference for each (ADR 020 cites its files and lines); one correction to the plan: an optional group does not "ship like any other", it ships only when `with` names it. Refused, naming the gem and what to do: a path gem outside the project that is not there or whose path ends in `.` or `..`, and a server that refuses the login. First released in `v0.0.1.pre.17`. Not yet: the musl and ARM64 Windows builds of the example (no C toolchain in their CI jobs, as for `examples/native_app`), `installed.yml` building the example from a tag, and `Gem.bindir` on an app's `PATH` (ADR 020, section 6).

### The installers developers expect **maybe 1.0**

**Why.** ADR 009 and ADR 010 left polish for when a developer needs it: a disk image's background and window layout; `diskutil image create`, which replaces `hdiutil create` on macOS 26 and newer; a per-machine NSIS install; closing a running copy before reinstalling; file associations and URL schemes; AppImage update information; `.msi`, `.deb`, and `.rpm`.

**What.** Each with the platform's own maintained tool, as ADR 010 requires, and only when a developer or a wrapper asks for it. Move to `diskutil` before `hdiutil create` stops working.

**Done when.** Each is decided for 1.0 or deferred with a reason here.

**Status.** Decided 2026-11-02 (requested): all are deferred past 1.0 but the `.deb`, which the maintainer asked for and is done (below). Nobody else, neither a developer nor a wrapper, has asked for any of them, and the plan makes each wait for that. What was checked instead is the one thing that could force a change, `hdiutil create`: on macOS 26.6.2 (the `macos-26` runner) and on macOS 27 (build 26A434) it exits 0 and makes an image `hdiutil verify` accepts and `hdiutil attach` mounts with the bundle and the `Applications` link, printing only a deprecation warning on standard error ("Please use 'diskutil image create'"), which `Fledge::Command` reads only when the command fails; macOS 15 has no `diskutil image create from --volumeName`, and its volume, unlike `hdiutil`'s HFS+, is APFS, so a move would change what a mounted image is for a warning. The move waits for the release that removes `hdiutil create` or for the oldest macOS Fledge supports to have `diskutil image create`; then `Fledge::Dmg.write` changes in one call, with a test that makes the image and attaches it. The rest, with the reason and the maintained tool that would carry each:
- **Disk image polish** (a background and window layout): create-dmg or dmgbuild, which run `hdiutil` or AppleScript against Finder and would put a dependency on a GUI session in a build; wait for a developer to ask.
- **Per-machine NSIS install** (`RequestExecutionLevel admin`, `$PROGRAMFILES64`): NSIS's own; it adds an elevation prompt and a second uninstall registry hive, and decision 18 and Tauri and electron-builder default to per-user.
- **Closing a running copy before reinstalling**: NSIS's `FindProcDLL` or `nsProcess` plugins, which Fledge's pinned NSIS does not carry; a plugin to audit and pin.
- **File associations and URL schemes**: `fledge.yml` keys and a registry or Info.plist entry on each system; they widen what developers control (decision 18), so a developer's request should shape them.
- **AppImage update information**: appimagetool's `--updateinformation` and `zsync`; it needs a place where the developer publishes, which Fledge does not know.
- **`.msi` and `.rpm`**: WiX and `rpmbuild`, each a platform's own maintained tool and each its own installer to deliver on a fresh machine; cost is a Delivery job and a pinned toolchain per format.
- **`.deb`**: done on 2026-11-02 at the maintainer's word (decisions, 2026-11-02), ADR 021: `dpkg-deb --build`, asked for with `installers: [appimage, deb]`, delivered by apt in Debian and Ubuntu containers on x86_64 and aarch64. First released in `v0.1.0`.

A wrapper (Tokra) asked on 2026-11-05 for more. The stories from "Requests from a wrapper: the design rule" on take what one general seam or a plain key can carry. These stay deferred, past 1.0, each until a developer has a reason:
- **`.msi`** (WiX): a second Windows installer for what the NSIS setup program does, whose reasons would be enterprise deployment through Group Policy or Intune, and a Store route the setup program already takes (`doc/contributors/research/2026-11-04-app-stores.md`); WiX needs the .NET SDK, a pinned toolchain per Windows platform, and a Delivery job. Not a seam.
- **A per-machine Windows install**: decision 18 and the prior art default to per user; a second mode doubles the install, uninstall, registration, and shortcut cases, and ADR 029, section 2 freezes where an installer puts the app. The NSIS include cannot do it, since it changes four of Fledge's settings together; it will be an opt-in key of its own, such as `windows: install_for`.
- **The Start menu folder, and a disk image's window, background, and license agreement**: the first is in Fledge's shortcut code, and the second is above.
- **An assembly hook** (`hooks`): see the design rule.
- **Declined**: extra files for a `.deb` or AppImage, and a whole-script NSIS template. Each makes Fledge's layout or generated text API. (A custom sign command, `.deb` maintainer scripts, and a `.deb` changelog were declined first and taken back on the wrapper's argument; see their stories.)

The rest are not worth doing before a developer asks.

### Requests from a wrapper: the design rule

A wrapper asked on 2026-11-05 for many packaging settings, key by key. A bundler that owns its packaging, as Tauri's does (prior art), has a key for each; ADR 029, section 4 makes each key Fledge adds something it keeps through all of 1.x, and a wrapper is a plug-in on Fledge: Fledge, the core, offers a few stable seams once and knows no plug-in's words. The next stories follow one rule. Common metadata that every packaging tool states (a description, a homepage, a license text) is a plain, neutral key. A setting that only one system's file can say is not a key per setting but one of three seams, each general and each small: **a fragment** in the system's own format, merged into the file Fledge writes (the data seam); **one NSIS include** for the Windows setup program, which is the only installer whose behavior is code (the install-time seam); and **the file-association key** ADR 032 designed, because it must change four files in step. A fourth seam, **an assembly hook** (a command the build runs on the assembled folder before it signs and archives, as Cargo's `build.rs` and Tauri's `beforeBundleCommand` run), is not taken: every request it could serve is served by the project's files or by a fragment, and the folder's layout would become API; add it when a developer has a case the others cannot meet, under the name `hooks`. A new output (`.rpm`, `.msi`) is not a seam and is weighed alone. The cost of each seam as a 1.x promise is stated in its story.

### A Linux build takes the icon sizes a developer has **maybe 1.0**

**Why.** A Linux build refuses a project that lacks any of `ICON-16.png`, `-32`, `-64`, `-128`, `-256`, and `-512` (`lib/fledge/icon.rb`, `desktop_entry.rb`, `app_image.rb`), though the Icon Theme Specification requires no particular set and a desktop scales the nearest size it finds (`doc/contributors/research/2026-11-05-app-metadata-and-installer-needs.md`, section 3). Tools that make icon sets write 32, 64, 128, 256, and 512 pixels, never 16, so their output fails today. A wrapper asked (Tokra, C1). Effort S. 1.x class: compatible, a refusal removed (ADR 029, section 4: "A new icon size for a target is optional").

**What.** A Linux build takes at least one of the six PNGs, ships those that exist, makes the AppImage's top icon the 256-pixel one or else the largest, and names the missing sizes on standard error. Fledge still reads no image: a size is the file name's claim (ADR 010, section 4), and a wrapper that holds a `128x128@2x.png` copies it to `ICON-256.png` itself. A project with all six builds what it builds today.

**Done when.** A project with only `-32`, `-128`, and `-256` builds, its `.deb` and AppImage hold three hicolor icons, `desktop-file-validate` accepts the entry, and a project with none fails with today's message.

**Status.** Done (requested), ADR 010, section 4, amended. `Fledge::Icon#linux` gives the PNGs there are, by size, and fails as a missing `-16` failed when there is none; the desktop entry and the `.deb` ship those; the AppImage's top icon is the 256-pixel PNG, else the largest, as Tauri's bundler picks the largest square; and `fledge build` names on standard error the files it lacks (`fledge build: warning: the icon has no icons/icon-16.png, ... so Linux desktops scale the nearest size there is`). The message for a project with none keeps its form but now says Linux takes at least one of the sizes, since "one PNG of each size" was no longer true. The Linux Build jobs prove it with `.github/scripts/check-icon-sizes.sh`: `examples/windowed_app` with three PNGs builds and warns, `dpkg-deb -c` and the AppImage's `--appimage-extract` each show three hicolor icons, `desktop-file-validate` accepts both desktop entries, and with no PNG the build exits 1 naming `icons/icon-16.png`. Not run on musl, whose Build job does the same build with the same code.

### What an app says about itself **maybe 1.0**

**Why.** Software centers, Task Manager, and Settings show a description and a homepage, and Fledge writes neither (`lib/fledge/desktop_entry.rb`, `deb.rb`, `windows_resources.rb`, `nsis/script.rb`). A wrapper asked (Tokra, A1 and A2). Effort S to M. 1.x class: compatible, new keys, and a project that sets none builds the same files byte for byte. Nothing is defaulted from the gemspec: a new default changes what a build writes (ADR 029, section 4).

**What.** `description` (one line) and `long_description`: the desktop entry's `Comment=`, the `.deb`'s `Description` (synopsis, then extended lines, Debian Policy 5.6.13), and the Windows program's `Comments` version string (rcedit). `homepage`: the `.deb`'s `Homepage` and the uninstall key's `URLInfoAbout` and `HelpLink`. These are keys, not fragments, because rcedit's strings and the uninstall key are written by Fledge's own tools and no fragment reaches them, and because the vocabulary is the same on every system. The category (A3) is not a key: macOS, the desktop entry, and the `.deb` each take a value from a different list that does not map one to one (`LSApplicationCategoryType`, `Categories=`, `Section`), so a wrapper that already has its own list writes each value into the fragment of that system (next story), and Fledge keeps no table.

**Done when.** The example app sets each; Delivery reads them back with `desktop-file-validate`, `dpkg-deb -f`, `(Get-Item).VersionInfo`, and the uninstall key; a project without them builds today's files. README, README.rdoc, schema, help header, and CHANGELOG say so.

**Status.** Done (requested), ADR 021, section 3, amended. `description` refuses a line break and `homepage` anything but an http or https URL with no space, quote, or angle bracket (Policy 5.6.23); `long_description` is free text. macOS gets neither: Apple's Information Property List Key Reference (its Core Foundation and Launch Services keys, read 2026-11-05) has no key for a description or a homepage, only `CFBundleName`, `CFBundleDisplayName`, and the category, which the next story leaves to a fragment. `examples/windowed_app` sets all three, and Delivery reads them back: `deliver-deb.sh` with `dpkg-deb --field` (`Description`, synopsis and extended lines, and `Homepage`) and the installed desktop entry's `Comment`; `deliver-windowed.sh` the folder's `Comment` on glibc and musl Linux beside `desktop-file-validate`; `deliver-windowed.ps1` `(Get-Item).VersionInfo.Comments`; `deliver-installer.ps1` the uninstall key's `URLInfoAbout` and `HelpLink`. The tests compare the files a project without the keys writes with today's. README.rdoc speaks of `fledge.yml` only in general and needed no change.

### A license text with the app **maybe 1.0**

**Why.** A Windows setup program shows a license page before it installs, and a `.deb` carries `/usr/share/doc/PACKAGE/copyright`. A wrapper asked (Tokra, A4). Effort M. 1.x class: compatible, a new key.

**What.** `license_file`, a path in the project, and `linux: deb_changelog`, a path to a changelog that Fledge gzips (`gzip -n -9`) to `/usr/share/doc/PACKAGE/changelog.gz`, the file a package with no separate upstream carries (Debian Policy 12.7; unverified here, the story reads it first): NSIS's license page (the file written with a byte order mark, as a Unicode installer needs) and the `.deb`'s copyright file. A `license` identifier key is not added: only an RPM takes one (Tauri, `linux/rpm.rs:61`), so it waits for that story. The disk image's license agreement is deferred: it is an `hdiutil` license resource the research could not verify (the note's section 1.4), and the disk image is about to change tools (see "The installers developers expect"); revisit with that change.

**Done when.** Delivery reads the page's text from the setup program on a fresh Windows computer (and `/S` shows none), and the copyright file from the installed `.deb`; a project without the key builds today's files.

### A fragment for each system's metadata **maybe 1.0**

**Why.** A wrapper asked for category (A3), a minimum macOS version above Fledge's floor and App Transport Security (D4), more `.deb` fields (D8), and the settings that a desktop entry takes. Named keys would be about a dozen (`category`, `minimum_version`, `exception_domain`, `depends`, `recommends`, `provides`, `conflicts`, `replaces`, `priority`, `section`, `categories`, `startup_wm_class`), each Fledge's for 1.x, and a thirteenth for each later request. Each of these is one line of the system's own file, in a format that system's standard owns and keeps stable. Effort M. 1.x class: compatible, new keys.

**What.** The data seam: a file in the project, in the system's own format, merged into the one Fledge writes. `macos: info_plist` (a property list, read with `plutil`, the system's tool; Fledge writes no plist parser), `linux: desktop_entry` (`Key=Value` lines for the `[Desktop Entry]` group, checked by `desktop-file-validate` in Delivery), and `linux: deb_control` (control fields, as `dpkg-deb` reads them). Contract. Inputs: the file's path in `fledge.yml`, relative to the project. When: while the file it merges into is written, so signing and archiving see the result. Fledge's own keys win: a key in the fragment that Fledge writes (identity, icon, executable, `CFBundleDocumentTypes` and `MimeType` from file associations, `Description` when `description` is set) fails the build, naming the key and the file. Two exceptions, each one line in the ADR: `LSMinimumSystemVersion`, which a fragment may raise but not below 14.0, Fledge's floor (ADR 029, section 6), and `Depends` in a `.deb`, whose entries are added after the ones Fledge computes from `system_libraries` (ADR 030), as a library opened with `dlopen` cannot be listed there. A malformed file fails the build naming it. Fledge checks no value: `LSApplicationCategoryType`, `Categories=`, and `Section` are the system's to validate, as `desktop-file-validate` and `plutil -lint` do in the wrapper's own checks. What this replaces: the dozen keys above, and the template keys a wrapper would otherwise ask for (a desktop file template makes Fledge's generated text API; a fragment does not). Cost as a frozen promise: three key names, the rule that Fledge's keys win, and the two exceptions; the files Fledge writes stay unfrozen (ADR 029, section 3), so a refactor of them stays free as long as the fragment still merges. Tested with real tools: `plutil` reads each key back and `codesign --verify --deep --strict` still passes on macOS; `desktop-file-validate` and `dpkg-deb -f` on Linux; a duplicate and a malformed file refused. What it cannot do: change a file Fledge writes by tool and not by text (rcedit's strings, the code signature), run anything at install time, or reach the AppImage, which has only the desktop entry; and extra files for a `.deb` or AppImage and maintainer scripts (a developer's script run as root at install, which Fledge cannot test) are not offered; a changelog and `Priority` are control-file lines or files outside the seam, and `Priority` is a field the fragment may set. Entitlements for the code signature wait for "Notarization and real trust chains", where YJIT's entitlement is settled (ADR 016); a `macos: entitlements` fragment of the same kind is the answer then. Reserve `macos`, `windows`, and the three keys in ADR 029, section 5 with this story.

**Done when.** The example app carries each fragment; Delivery reads its keys back with `plutil`, `desktop-file-validate`, and `dpkg-deb -f`; a duplicate of a Fledge key, a minimum version below 14.0, and a malformed file are each refused naming the key or the file; a project without fragments builds today's files. An ADR records the alternatives (keys; templates; Tauri's `infoPlist` merge, which is this seam; nfpm's per-field overrides).

### A Windows setup program takes one NSIS include **maybe 1.0**

**Why.** A wrapper asked for something the setup program must do at install time: install a component first (B1, the web view runtime a window needs on a Windows 10 computer that lacks it), refuse to install over a newer version (D6), and set the installer's look and compression (D3). The Windows setup program is the one installer whose behavior is code, so a data fragment cannot carry it. Named keys would be `prerequisites` (a list with its own object), `allow_downgrade`, two images, an icon, languages, compression, and a Start menu folder. Effort M to L. 1.x class: compatible, a new key.

**What.** The install-time seam: `windows: nsis_include`, a path to an NSIS script in the project. Contract. When: `makensis` includes it once, in two documented places the ADR names and no more: near the top, where Fledge has defined `NAME`, `VERSION`, `IDENTIFIER`, and `PUBLISHER` and has not yet set the things the include may override (compression, the interface's images and icon, the languages), which Fledge sets with `!ifndef` after it; and as a macro Fledge calls, if defined, right before it copies the app and after the old version's uninstaller has run, so an update that finds a component present does nothing. Failure: a script `makensis` rejects fails the build with its message and the file's name; a macro that calls `Abort` stops the install, as Tauri's section does. Fledge keeps `RequestExecutionLevel user`, the install folder, and the uninstall key (ADR 029, section 2); an include that sets them is its author's mistake that Delivery cannot catch, and the ADR says so. A prerequisite is then a few NSIS lines the wrapper writes once (registry value non-empty, `File` of a bundled installer, `ExecWait`, `Abort`), and the wrapper supplies the file and its pin; Fledge ships nothing of Microsoft's, and whether a wrapper may redistribute a Microsoft installer is that wrapper's to read. What this replaces: the keys above, six to ten of them. Cost as a frozen promise: one key, the two insertion points, the four names, and that Fledge keeps the NSIS 3 language it pins (a minor NSIS update in 1.x is allowed, as 3.x keeps its language compatible; **unverified**, to check against NSIS's changelog before the ADR). Compared with the declarative `prerequisites` list the research proposed, the include is less to promise (one key, not an object) and covers more, and the list is easier to test and cannot break `/S`; the include gives that up, and the ADR says that an include's silence under `/S` is its author's responsibility, while Fledge's example proves the seam with a fake prerequisite. Tauri's `installerHooks` and `template` and electron-builder's `include` are the prior art; a whole-script template (Tauri's `template`) is not offered, since it would make Fledge's generated script API. What it cannot do: a per-machine install (D7), which changes `RequestExecutionLevel`, the install folder, the registry hive, and the uninstall key together, and is a key of its own, deferred; the Start menu folder, which Fledge's shortcut code writes; and signing, which stays `--sign`.

**Done when.** On each Windows platform Fledge builds for, Delivery installs an app whose include has a fake prerequisite (a program that sets a registry value): a second install skips it; a failing one stops the install naming it; `/S` shows no window; an include that is not NSIS fails the build naming the file; a project without the key builds today's script. An ADR weighs the seam against the declarative list, against WiX Burn's chain and Inno Setup's `[Run]`, and against the included hook, and says which NSIS plug-ins an include may use (only the ones the pinned NSIS carries).

### The files an app opens **maybe 1.0**

**Why.** An app that edits or views files wants the system to offer it for them. A wrapper asked (Tokra) and the research found it in scope (decisions, "Drag to fuse"). ADR 032, section 3 designed it as `file_associations`, and ADR 029, section 5 reserves the name. This story is steps 5 and 6 of "Build from a built app, and the files an app opens", taken out so that it does not wait for `--from`. Effort M to L. 1.x class: compatible, a new key.

**What.** Build ADR 032, section 3 as written. It stays a key, and not three fragments, because it must change four files in step: `CFBundleDocumentTypes` (rank `Alternate`) on macOS; the registration under `HKCU\Software\Classes` and its removal on uninstall on Windows; and `MimeType=` together with `%F` in `Exec=` on Linux, which a fragment cannot do because Fledge owns `Exec`. **Done when.** As the story it came from says: the registration reads back from each system's own files, and an SDL3 test app, not a wrapper, receives `open -a App.app FOLDER` on macOS and a path in `ARGV` on Windows and Linux.

### A Windows signing command **after 1.0**

**Why.** A developer whose certificate is in a cloud or hardware service signs with that service's own tool: Azure Trusted Signing through `signtool` and Microsoft's plug-in, DigiCert KeyLocker, SSL.com eSigner, or a key in a cloud KMS through `jsign`, `osslsigncode`, or `relic`. `--sign THUMBPRINT` (ADR 016) reaches only a certificate in Windows' store. A wrapper asked (Tokra, D3); this story takes back the decline of its first answer, since a seam answers every service with no code for any of them, where a story for each service would be code for each. Prior art: electron-builder's `sign` and Tauri's `signCommand`. Effort M. 1.x class: compatible, a new option.

**What.** The signing seam: `--sign-command COMMAND` (and the environment variable `FLEDGE_SIGN_COMMAND`), not a `fledge.yml` key, because the identity and its credentials stay on the developer's computer (ADR 016, section 1). **Done when.** A stand-in signer on a Windows runner, which records each path it is given and writes a marker the test reads back, is run once per file in the order above and a failing one stops the build; `--sign` and `--sign-command` together are a usage error.

### One project, several programs **1.0**

**Why.** An app can have several ways in that share one codebase: a command-line program, a terminal program, and a graphical one, so a player can save in one and load in another. `fledge build` makes one program per run today, so three programs would be three copies of the project and the runtime. **Done when.** An example with a console program and a windowed program sharing one project builds and delivers on every desktop platform, and a project with no section builds as before.

**Status.** Done (requested), ADR 024: `trunk`'s CI run 41584541749 delivers `examples/programs` on `arm64-darwin`, `x64-mingw-ucrt`, and `aarch64-mingw-ucrt` as well as on Linux and musl, every job green. ### Fledge on RubyGems.org, and runtimes that do not depend on one host **1.0**

**Why.** From 1.0.0 Fledge is also released on RubyGems.org, by a human; a git tag and a GitHub Release stay, and a release can be found either way (decisions, 2026-11-03, with the maintainer's explanation of 2026-11-04). Before 1.0 nothing is pushed there: the maintainer will not claim a name for a project that may fail. Fledge is one gem, pure Ruby, so there is one `gem push`. Its runtimes are Fledge's own builds (ADR 001, 003, 004, 011, 017), not upstream's files, and today every runtime and every LGPL source bundle that an app's `notices/NOTICES.txt` names lives only as a GitHub Release asset (ADR 004, section 5). **What.** (1) Publish: a checklist for the one `gem push`, and the port of RatatuiRuby's version and git tooling, copied and not depended on (ADR 028, section 7): `rake bump:*`, `rake release`'s replacement of Bundler's normalized prerelease tag with the SemVer one, `release:update_stable`, and the lookup that finds the CI run for a tagged commit. Copied code is the maintainer's own projects' and sits in SPDX snippets (AGPL-3.0-or-later, AGENTS.md, Licensing). `rake release` stays refusing to push until 1.0. (2) Make the runtimes' host replaceable: every runtime is already checked against the pins the gem carries (ADR 014), so any host serves it. Add an environment variable that names another place to download from, as `PYTHON_BUILD_MIRROR_URL` and `RUSTUP_DIST_SERVER` do for their tools (ADR 002, section 2), and a second default location, once the maintainer names a host. (3) Decide with the maintainer how the LGPL source offer outlives a host: ship the sources of libxcrypt, GMP, and libfuse with each runtime's notices, or point them at upstream's own archives by pinned hash (LGPL-2.1, section 6; `questions.md`).

Not taken: a platform gem per target that carries its runtime, as RatatuiRuby's and nokogiri's gems carry compiled code, which was built and proved on `experiment/platform-gems` (run 41959876834, all seven desktop platforms) and is kept as a later option under one gem name if offline installs matter. **Done when.** The one gem builds from a release commit and installs and builds an app on every platform; the maintainer can publish it with a documented checklist; `fledge build` takes its runtimes from a place named by an environment variable, checked against the same pins; and the LGPL source offer does not name a host that is the only place it can be had.

**Status.** (2) is done (requested), ADR 031: `FLEDGE_RUNTIME_MIRROR` names a server that holds a copy of the releases as `vVERSION/NAME`, every download from it is checked against the gem's pins, and a file it lacks fails the build rather than coming from the release, as uv and rustup treat their mirrors. The second default location waits for the maintainer to name a host. (3) is done (requested), ADR 004, section 5, amended: each Linux and Windows runtime, and so each app, carries the pinned sources of its LGPL libraries and of the installer tools whose licenses ask for source in `notices/sources/`, the license audit accounts for them, and NOTICES.txt names that folder before the release's bundle, which stays. Agents took the first of the two ways the story named, since it needs no host and LGPL-2.1, section 6(a), names it first; pointing at upstream's archives would have kept a host the only place. (2) and (3) were first released in `v0.5.0`. (1) is not started.

### Build from a built app, and the files an app opens **maybe 1.0**

**Why.** The maintainer's entry on drag-to-fuse (decisions.md, which applies it as "Drag to fuse"): a command line is a barrier for the newcomers a framework built on Fledge is for, and a framework's way to make an app by dropping a folder on another app is in scope. A single Windows file (ADR 032, option A) and building for another system (`questions.md` D) wait.

**Done when.** On `arm64-darwin`, `x64-mingw-ucrt`, and `x86_64-linux`: an app built with `build_tools` runs `fledge build --from` on itself for another project on a fresh runner with no Ruby, compiler, or network; the result delivers on another fresh runner as `fledge build`'s app of the same project would (identity read back with each system's tools, `codesign --verify --deep --strict` on macOS, its installers installed when asked for); a project with a Gemfile, an APP of another release or platform, and a folder Fledge did not build are refused with the statuses ADR 032 names; `file_associations` reads back from each system's own files; and the help, schema, README, and ADR 032 (Accepted) say what a framework may rely on.

**Status.** Designed (requested), ADR 032, Draft. The spike is done: `experiment/drag-to-fuse`, run 41978615197, every job green. Inside an app built with `fledge build`, a script using only Ruby's standard library and the system's tools copied the app with another project in place, from a folder and from a zip, on fresh macOS and Windows runners with no Ruby reachable, in 1.4 to 2.3 s; the result ran and read its files through `__dir__`; on macOS `codesign --verify --deep --strict` accepted the copy sealed again ad hoc; on Windows the program kept the original's version information, which needs rcedit; a folder opened with the macOS app through LaunchServices did not reach `ARGV`; Windows' `tar.exe` read a zip appended to an exe.

### 1.0 **1.0**

**Why.** 1.0 tells developers the CLI, `fledge.yml`, and what a built app contains will not change without a major version.

**What.** When at least some of the unproven things are proven (a real iPhone or iPad and App Review, a real signing trust chain, Android on arm64), every other **1.0** story is done, the money-bound ones that are not proven are named as unproven in the README, and every **maybe 1.0** story is decided, review the CLI and `fledge.yml` for anything to break first, prepare `1.0.0` as a gem release for a human to publish on RubyGems.org (decisions, 2026-11-03 and 2026-11-04; the release is also found by its git tag and GitHub Release, which stay), and stop treating pre-1.0 breaking changes as free (AGENTS.md). Until then agents release by tag and GitHub Release, as ordinary 0.MINOR.PATCH versions, beginning with `v0.1.0` (decisions, 2026-11-02); the `0.0.1.pre.N` form ended at `v0.0.1.pre.17`. From `1.0.0`, releases follow ADR 028, section 7: a `release/1.0` branch cut from `trunk` with the ported `rake bump:*` tooling (the story above), backports by `git cherry-pick -x`, a SemVer tag on every release, `stable` at the newest, and the maintainer running `rake release`.

**Done when.** At least some of the three money-bound proofs are done, the gems of `1.0.0` are ready to publish and have passed an install-and-deliver check on every platform, and the maintainer has published them.

**Status.** The review of the CLI and `fledge.yml` is done (requested): `doc/contributors/research/2026-11-04-one-point-oh-surface.md`. A windowed program under `programs` with no name takes the project's (ADR 024, section 1). The help reserves the bare words Fledge reads as platforms (ADR 029, section 5). `runtime/licenses.yml`, `doc/licensing.md`, and the README count `bin/ruby` among the files that hold Fledge's code. All six are done, first released in `v0.5.0`; what is left of the story is the rest of its **What**.
