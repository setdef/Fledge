# ADR 007: An App's Gems Are Its Developer's, Licenses and All

## Context and Problem Statement

Decision 8 keeps Fledge's runtime free of copyleft obligations beyond notices (`decisions.md`, 2026-10-26). Agents first applied it to everything `fledge build` copies into an app, the app's own gems included: a GPL, AGPL, or MPL gem, one with an unknown license, and one that declared none failed the build; an LGPL gem shipped only with its exact source pinned; every compiled file was read for known copyleft libraries, and every Rust crate with `cargo metadata`; and each app carried `notices/GEMS.txt`, Fledge's account of each gem's license. Should Fledge judge the gems an app depends on by their licenses?

The maintainer answered, on 2026-10-28 (`decisions.md`): "Why does it refuse GPL and AGPL gems? That's up to the app dev to decide, not up to Fledge. In fact, why is Fledge taking any kind of opinion at all about what license the gems the app dev uses are under?"

## Decision Drivers

- A gem is in the Gemfile because the developer put it there; whether its license suits the app is the developer's call.
- Decision 8 protects developers from obligations Fledge adds on its own, through its runtime, which no developer chose and none can remove.
- Code is a liability (`decisions.md`, 2026-10-28).

## Considered Options

1. Judge no gem by its license: ship every gem the lockfile locks, and write no notices of Fledge's own for them.
2. The license regime Fledge had: the runtime's license policy applied to the app's gems, reviewed maps of compiled code, `fledge.yml`'s `licenses` and `compiled` keys, and `notices/GEMS.txt`.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen: option 1, because the maintainer's words rule out any opinion on a gem's license. `fledge build` ships every gem the lockfile locks for the target, whatever its license, whether or not it declares one, and whatever its compiled files hold. Each gem ships as RubyGems or Bundler installs it, with its own files, license files among them. Each app's `notices/` holds only what Fledge put there: its runtime's `NOTICES.txt` and SBOM.

The prior art agrees. PyInstaller says what it builds "can be shipped with whatever license you want, as long as it complies with the licenses of your dependencies" (`doc/license.rst`, lines 15 to 17, at 4469721) and writes no notices for them. Tauri's bundler takes only the app's own license (`crates/tauri-bundler/src/bundle/settings.rs`, lines 651 to 655, at 32576120f). Warbler packs gems as they are installed, with no license handling (`jruby/warbler` at 3196d5f). Bundler's `bundle licenses` prints each declared license as information, with no verdict (`bundler/cli.rb`, lines 558 to 570, at Bundler 4.0.20).

Confidence: high. This is the maintainer's decision, and the examples ship gems the old regime refused.

### Consequences

- Good, because a class of build failures that had nothing to do with whether the app runs is gone, and with it about 2,000 lines of code, maps, and tests, and a map of reviewed gems that went stale with every gem release.
- Good, because the runtime keeps its own audit (ADR 004), so a developer still gets a runtime with no copyleft obligation beyond notices; whether the app as a whole has one depends on the gems they chose.
- Bad, because Fledge no longer warns that a precompiled gem compiles in an LGPL library (nokogiri's macOS and Windows builds say so in their own `LICENSE-DEPENDENCIES.md`); the gem's own notices are where that fact lives.
- Neutral, because `fledge.yml` no longer has `licenses` or `compiled`, and a file that still has either fails as any unknown key does (ADR 044).
- Neutral, because `fledge build` still checks that each gem runs where the app goes and that its bytes are the ones locked. Those checks decide whether the app works, never what a gem is licensed under: `doc/contributors/design/gems.md`, "Checks that are not about licenses".

### Confirmation

`test/gem_app_build_test.rb` builds a GPL gem, an AGPL native gem shaped like Tokra, a gem that declares no license, an LGPL gem without its source, an MPL gem, one whose license SPDX does not know, and a native gem with GNU libiconv compiled in, and requires all of them to ship without a word. `examples/native_app` ships spreadsheet (GPL-3.0), and `examples/nokogiri_app` ships for Windows with GNU libiconv linked in, built and delivered in CI. CI's Licenses jobs still plant a GPL gem in each runtime and require the runtime's audit to fail: that gem would be Fledge's, not the developer's.

## More Information

`doc/licensing.md`, "Your app's own gems", tells developers where their gems' notices are and that `bundle licenses` lists the declared licenses.
