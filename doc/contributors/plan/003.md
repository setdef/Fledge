# Plan 003: The Rest of the Way to 1.0

## Status

Planned (requested). Agents wrote this plan and may reorder or rewrite it (AGENTS.md, Questions). Plan 002 is done: every target it planned builds on its own system and delivers on a fresh computer, and what it left unproven is gathered here, from the "Not yet" lines of its stories and of ADRs 005, 006, 008 to 012, 015, 016, and 018, and from `doc/contributors/questions.md`. Each story names the maintainer's decision it serves (`doc/contributors/decisions.md`), why it matters, and when it is done.

A story marked **1.0** must be done before 1.0; one marked **maybe 1.0** is decided when 1.0 is near; one marked **0.2.0** is the maintainer's priority for that release (decisions, 2026-11-02); one marked **after 1.0** is recorded so it is not lost. Three stories wait on money (accounts, certificates, and a way to run Android's arm64 build) and say so; work proceeds around them. The maintainer ruled on 2026-11-02 that 1.0 waits until at least some of those proofs exist, and that releases move from `0.0.1.pre.N` to ordinary 0.MINOR.PATCH versions, beginning with 0.1.0 (decisions, 2026-11-02).

## Stories

### A framework wrapper ships an app through Fledge **1.0**

**Why.** Lota (2D games), Tokra (desktop apps), and Rooibos (terminal UIs) are meant to call Fledge, and Fledge's design is for the developers who use them. Wrappers depend on Fledge; Fledge depends on no wrapper's repository (decisions, 2026-11-02). So the story is Fledge's side: that an app built on a wrapper's gems ships through `fledge build`, and that what a wrapper may rely on is written down.

**What.** Pick the wrapper whose app is closest to shipping, pin Fledge from a release tag in its Gemfile, and build and deliver one of its examples on each platform it supports with that release's runtimes. Fix what breaks in Fledge, and record in an ADR what a wrapper may rely on: the command, `fledge.yml`, exit statuses, and output.

**Done when.** An app built on a wrapper, with a tagged Fledge, runs on a fresh runner of each platform the wrapper supports, with no Ruby there, and an ADR states what a wrapper may rely on.

**Status.** Done on Fledge's side (requested), ADR 019. Rooibos is the wrapper closest to shipping (published, pure Ruby on a precompiled Rust gem, examples that need only a terminal; Tokra needs a webview and a display, and Lota is unpublished with no examples). Its website's first app, built from a project that pins Fledge `v0.0.1.pre.16` by tag with the release's runtimes, runs in a terminal on a fresh runner with no Ruby on `arm64-darwin`, `x86_64-linux`, `aarch64-linux`, and `x64-mingw-ucrt` (the `experiment/wrapper` branch, run 41360640888): the first screen, a typed key, Ctrl-C, and exit status 0. Building Rooibos's repository found a bug in Fledge, fixed: a locked gem with a RubyGems plugin whose loader the runtime has (`rdoc`, which `irb` asks for) failed the build; first released in `v0.0.1.pre.17`. ADR 019 records what a wrapper may rely on. Whether a wrapper's own repository adds CI of this shape, or builds Tokra's and Lota's apps, is that wrapper's to decide; Fledge waits on none of it. The unsent drafts in `doc/contributors/drafts/` tell Rooibos and Tokra, optionally, how to depend on a tag.

### iOS on a real device, and App Review **1.0, waits on money**

**Why.** The maintainer asked for iOS and iPadOS before 1.0 (decisions, 2026-10-27) and for an Apple Developer account at the last responsible moment (2026-10-28). The Simulator is not the device: Apple's sandbox on an iPhone differs (ADR 011, section 6), and App Review judges an embedded interpreter by guidelines 2.5.2 and 4.2.6.

**What.** When the maintainer opens the account (the maintainer ruled on 2026-11-02 that no money is spent now; decisions, 2026-11-02): sign `examples/hello` and `examples/mobile_app` with a development team, run them on a real iPhone and iPad (a device cloud for CI), and submit one to TestFlight.

**Done when.** A Fledge-built app runs on a real iPhone and iPad, and App Review accepts one into TestFlight.

### Notarization and real trust chains **1.0, waits on money**

**Why.** Decision 17 asks that developers who have done Apple's and Microsoft's ceremony be able to sign so that neither system blocks their app. `fledge build --sign` signs with a developer's identity (ADR 016), but only a self-signed certificate has proven it.

**What.** When the maintainer opens the accounts (no money is spent now; decisions, 2026-11-02): `--notarize`, with notarytool and an App Store Connect API key, then stapler on the disk image; Azure Trusted Signing's signtool options; and Delivery jobs that check Gatekeeper's verdict (`spctl --assess`) and SmartScreen's on a downloaded copy. Find out whether YJIT still needs `com.apple.security.cs.allow-unsigned-executable-memory` under a Developer ID.

**Done when.** A notarized disk image opens on a fresh Mac with no warning, and a setup program signed through a real chain installs on a fresh Windows runner with no SmartScreen block.

### Android on arm64 **1.0, waits on money**

**Why.** Almost every Android phone is arm64-v8a, and CI runs that library on nothing: GitHub's runners emulate only x86-64 Android (ADR 011 and ADR 012). One manual run on an Apple-silicon Mac is not a gate.

**What.** Run the Mobile apps checks on an arm64-v8a emulator in CI, on an ARM64 runner with hardware virtualization if one becomes available, or by any other free means; no money is spent on it (decisions, 2026-11-02).

**Done when.** Every push runs the parity probe and the HTTPS script on arm64-v8a.

**Status.** Waiting on a free way to run it (requested): the maintainer ruled on 2026-11-02 that money is not spent on this now (decisions, 2026-11-02), so no device cloud, paid runner, or machine. Tried 2026-11-02 on `experiment/android-arm64`: no free GitHub-hosted runner (`ubuntu-24.04-arm`, `macos-14`, `macos-15`, `macos-26`, `windows-11-arm`) boots an arm64-v8a emulator with hardware acceleration, and Google publishes no emulator for Linux ARM64 or Windows ARM64 hosts (ADR 011, Consequences). Work proceeds on the default: the arm64-v8a library is built and its APK checked in CI, and the checks run on x86_64 emulators. Recheck when GitHub announces nested virtualization on an ARM64 runner.

### The gems real apps lock **1.0**

**Why.** Decision 20 and ADR 005 promise that an app's code runs as under `bundle exec`. Some Gemfiles still cannot ship: path gems outside the project, gem servers that need credentials or mirrors (`BUNDLE_MIRROR__*`), git submodules, path gems with extensions, optional groups (which ship like any other), and gem executables in `bin/` (ADR 005 and ADR 006).

**What.** Take each in turn, Bundler's own behavior as the reference: read its settings and credentials as Bundler reads them, check out submodules, and compile path gems as other gems are compiled. Refuse what still cannot work with a message that says why.

**Done when.** An example that uses each of these builds and delivers on every desktop platform, or Fledge refuses it with a message that names the gem and what to do.

**Status.** Done for the desktop platforms CI builds native gems on (requested), ADR 020. `examples/real_gems` uses every kind in one app and builds and delivers on `arm64-darwin`, `x86_64-linux`, `aarch64-linux`, and `x64-mingw-ucrt`: a gem through a loopback mirror that wants a login (Bundler's own mirror and credential settings), a path gem from a folder outside the project, a path gem with a C extension, a git gem with a submodule, an optional group the project's `with` asks for, and a gem's executable run with no Ruby. Bundler's own behavior is the reference for each (ADR 020 cites its files and lines); one correction to the plan: an optional group does not "ship like any other", it ships only when `with` names it. Refused, naming the gem and what to do: a path gem outside the project that is not there or whose path ends in `.` or `..`, and a server that refuses the login. First released in `v0.0.1.pre.17`. Not yet: the musl and ARM64 Windows builds of the example (no C toolchain in their CI jobs, as for `examples/native_app`), `installed.yml` building the example from a tag, and `Gem.bindir` on an app's `PATH` (ADR 020, section 6).

### The installers developers expect **maybe 1.0**

**Why.** ADR 009 and ADR 010 left polish for when a developer needs it: a disk image's background and window layout; `diskutil image create`, which replaces `hdiutil create` on macOS 26 and newer; a per-machine NSIS install; closing a running copy before reinstalling; file associations and URL schemes; AppImage update information; `.msi`, `.deb`, and `.rpm`.

**What.** Each with the platform's own maintained tool, as ADR 010 requires, and only when a developer or a wrapper asks for it. Move to `diskutil` before `hdiutil create` stops working.

**Done when.** Each is decided for 1.0 or deferred with a reason here.

**Status.** Decided 2026-11-02 (requested): all are deferred past 1.0 but the `.deb`, which the maintainer asked for and is done (below). Nobody else, neither a developer nor a wrapper, has asked for any of them, and the plan makes each wait for that. What was checked instead is the one thing that could force a change, `hdiutil create`: on macOS 26.6.2 (the `macos-26` runner) and on macOS 27 (build 26A434) it exits 0 and makes an image `hdiutil verify` accepts and `hdiutil attach` mounts with the bundle and the `Applications` link, printing only a deprecation warning on standard error ("Please use 'diskutil image create'"), which `Fledge::Command` reads only when the command fails; macOS 15 has no `diskutil image create from --volumeName`, and its volume, unlike `hdiutil`'s HFS+, is APFS, so a move would change what a mounted image is for a warning. The move waits for the release that removes `hdiutil create` or for the oldest macOS Fledge supports to have `diskutil image create`; then `Fledge::Dmg.write` changes in one call, with a test that makes the image and attaches it. The rest, with the reason and the maintained tool that would carry each:
- **Disk image polish** (a background and window layout): create-dmg or dmgbuild, which run `hdiutil` or AppleScript against Finder and would put a dependency on a GUI session in a build; wait for a developer to ask.
- **Per-machine NSIS install** (`RequestExecutionLevel admin`, `$PROGRAMFILES64`): NSIS's own; it adds an elevation prompt and a second uninstall registry hive, and decision 18 and Tauri and electron-builder default to per-user.
- **Closing a running copy before reinstalling**: NSIS's `FindProcDLL` or `nsProcess` plugins, which Fledge's pinned NSIS does not carry; a plugin to audit and pin.
- **File associations and URL schemes**: `fledge.yml` keys and a registry or Info.plist entry on each system; they widen what developers control (decision 18), so a developer's request should shape them.
- **AppImage update information**: appimagetool's `--updateinformation` and `zsync`; it needs a place where the developer publishes, which Fledge does not know.
- **`.msi` and `.rpm`**: WiX and `rpmbuild`, each a platform's own maintained tool and each its own installer to deliver on a fresh machine; cost is a Delivery job and a pinned toolchain per format.
- **`.deb`**: done on 2026-11-02 at the maintainer's word (decisions, 2026-11-02), ADR 021: `dpkg-deb --build`, asked for with `installers: [appimage, deb]`, delivered by apt in Debian and Ubuntu containers on x86_64 and aarch64. First released in `v0.1.0`.

The rest are not worth doing before a developer asks.

### Closing a Windows console **maybe 1.0**

**Why.** ADR 015 proves Ctrl-C and a hung-up terminal end a console app as they end `ruby main.rb`, but not closing a Windows console window, which GitHub's runners would not do from a script. The launcher leaves CTRL_CLOSE_EVENT to Windows' default handler, as `ruby.exe` does.

**What.** Find a scripted way to close a console window that GitHub's runners honor (ADR 015, section 2, records three that did not: `SC_CLOSE`, `WM_CLOSE`, and stopping `conhost.exe`), and compare the app with `ruby.exe`.

**Done when.** Delivery closes a console window on Windows and the app's `ensure` and `at_exit` run as `ruby.exe`'s do, or ADR 015 records why no runner can check it.

**Status.** Decided, and done for 1.0 (requested), ADR 015, section 2. The correction to the plan: when Windows closes a console, `ruby.exe` does not run `ensure` or `at_exit` either; Windows's default handler ends it at once with 0xC000013A, so "run as `ruby.exe`'s do" means they do not run. No real console window can be closed from a script on GitHub's runners, but a pseudoconsole can: `deliver-console.ps1` calls `ClosePseudoConsole`, as Windows Terminal does for a closing tab, and requires the built app (Delivery) and `ruby.exe` (Test) to end with 0xC000013A and a log with no `ensure` or `at_exit`, on `windows-latest` and `windows-11-arm`. The launcher needed no change. Not proven: a real window's close button, which sends the same event. First released in `v0.0.1.pre.17`.

### Native gems on phones **0.2.0**

**Why.** The maintainer made iPadOS, iOS, and Android the priorities for 0.2.0 (decisions, 2026-11-02), and Lota, a game engine that is a C++ extension over SDL3 and other libraries, is the first expected consumer: the mobile runtimes shipped pure-Ruby gems only (ADR 012), because a phone's Ruby loads no extension from a file (ADR 011, section 4).

**What.** Compile an app's gems' C and C++ extensions (mkmf's `extconf.rb`) for each target of the phone, with Xcode on a Mac and the NDK on a Mac or Linux, link them into the app, and register them so `require` finds them (ADR 022). Refuse what that does not cover, naming the gem. Write down how a host that owns the main loop (SDL3's `SDL_main`) coexists with `fledge_ruby_run_app`, and what an extension author provides.

**Done when.** An app whose gem has a C and C++ extension runs in the iOS Simulator on an iPhone and an iPad and on an x86_64 Android emulator, with arm64-v8a built and its APK checked, and the ADR says what was and was not verified.

**Status.** Done for C and C++ extensions (requested), ADR 022; `experiment/phones` run 41492629986, every job green: `examples/native_phone` runs in the iOS Simulator on an iPhone and an iPad and on x86_64 Android emulators with 4 KB and 16 KB pages, and its arm64-v8a library is built and its APK checked. First released in `v0.2.1`. Not yet, by decision or for want of money: a real iPhone or iPad, App Review, and arm64-v8a on an emulator (the same three as above); an SDL3 app on a phone (the optional spike); Rust extensions; Windows as the host of an Android build with extensions; `require_relative` of an extension.

### A game plays on a tablet and a phone **0.2.0**

**Why.** The maintainer's litmus test for 0.2.0, "phones and tablets", is playtesting a game written in Ruby using Lota on an M1 iPad Pro running iPadOS 27 and on a OnePlus Nord N30 5G running Android 13, built through Fledge (decisions, 2026-11-02). The iPad is an arm64 device Fledge has never run on; the phone is an arm64-v8a device, the build CI cannot run. Native gems with a simple C and C++ extension run in the iOS Simulator and on x86_64 Android emulators (ADR 022), but Lota is a C++ extension over SDL3, which owns the main loop and needs its own libraries built for each iOS slice and Android ABI.

**What.** Fledge's side: an SDL3 spike that builds SDL3 for the phone's targets from a pinned source, hosts a small SDL3 program as a gem with an `extconf.rb` extension, and shows an SDL window with touch input and a Ruby-driven loop in the iOS Simulator and on an Android emulator, with the main-thread and stack questions in ADR 022 answered by running them. Then how a project supplies a host (SDL's startup files and Activity), which the `host` key in `fledge.yml` answers (ADR 025). Lota's side, which Lota's session owns: an entry point Fledge can build, and an extension its `extconf.rb` builds. Fledge depends on no wrapper's repository, so the spike uses an SDL3 test gem, not Lota.

**Status.** The SDL3 spike is done (requested), ADR 025: `examples/sdl_phone` draws a frame loop Ruby drives and takes a tap in the iOS Simulator on an iPhone and an iPad and on x86_64 Android emulators (`experiment/sdl-phones`), with Ruby on the main thread inside `SDL_RunApp` on iOS and an `SDLActivity` running Ruby on a 64 MB thread on Android. `fledge build` now links an `extconf.rb`'s static libraries per iOS slice, keeps frameworks whole, and carries the shared libraries it links into the APK. Waiting: the maintainer's answer to `questions.md` Lota's side, and the playtest on devices (the `host` key that supplies the host is done, ADR 025). The linking and the `host` key were first released in `v0.2.1`. The playtest's steps, and what agents checked of it without a device, are in `doc/contributors/device-test.md`.

**Done when.** The maintainer playtests a game written in Ruby using Lota on that iPad and that phone, with a build the maintainer made through `fledge build ios` and `fledge build android` (a free Apple ID can sign a build for a device its owner holds; an arm64 Android phone runs the arm64-v8a build, which CI cannot).

### One project, several programs **1.0**

**Why.** An app can have several ways in that share one codebase: a command-line program, a terminal program, and a graphical one, so a player can save in one and load in another. `fledge build` makes one program per run today, so three programs would be three copies of the project and the runtime. The maintainer approved the design below (2026-11-02, in conversation) and asked for it before 1.0, because it changes `fledge.yml`, which 1.0 freezes.

**What.** A `fledge.yml` section that names each program, its path and its kind (console or windowed). `fledge build` builds all of them into one folder that shares one runtime, one copy of the project, and one set of gems. A windowed program gets its platform's installer entry; a console program sits beside it. Settle in an ADR first: the section's keys, how the launcher picks the program (one launcher per program, as a renamed copy, or one launcher and an argument), how the identity keys (`name`, `identifier`, `icon`) apply to each program, and what a project with no section does (it builds as it does today). Lota's `lota fuse` will call Fledge for its GUI program, which is a Lota entry point, not a shell script; check the design against that use.

**Done when.** An example with a console program and a windowed program sharing one project builds and delivers on every desktop platform, and a project with no section builds as before.

**Status.** Done (requested), ADR 024: `trunk`'s CI run 41584541749 delivers `examples/programs` on `arm64-darwin`, `x64-mingw-ucrt`, and `aarch64-mingw-ucrt` as well as on Linux and musl, every job green. Before that, on the `experiment/programs` branch (runs 41513512642 and 41515504473), `examples/programs` builds on all seven desktop platforms and delivers on `x86_64-linux`, `aarch64-linux`, `x86_64-linux-musl`, and `aarch64-linux-musl`, and its built-app test passes on Linux, macOS, and both Windows; its Windows Delivery stopped in `deliver-programs.ps1`, on an empty list PowerShell's strict mode would not count, after the console program had run, a script bug fixed since, and its macOS Delivery waited for a runner. `programs` in `fledge.yml` lists each program's `path`, `name`, `kind`, `identifier`, and `icon`; the project keeps `name`, `version`, `publisher`, `copyright`, and `installers`. Each program is a renamed copy of the launcher, and `fledge.cfg` gives each a section that the launcher finds by its own file name. At most one program is windowed (a second would need a second runtime in a second macOS bundle), and the installers are its; on macOS the console programs live in its bundle's `Contents/MacOS`, linked from the folder. A file `programs` lists builds that program alone; any other file is refused. `examples/programs` is a windowed and a console program sharing a save; in run 41515504473 every other example builds as before and delivers as before on Linux and musl, in macOS's console Delivery, and on Windows up to that step. Not yet, until a developer asks: several windowed programs, a console program on a `PATH` from an installer, and an AppImage that runs a console program by the name it is called with. A wrapper's `lota fuse` names its entry point as one of the programs, or builds it alone by its path (ADR 019). First released in `v0.2.1`.

### Windows on ARM64 without emulation **after 1.0**

**Why.** The ARM64 Windows runtime's tools (rcedit, NSIS, and LLVM's `llvm-readobj`) are x86 and x64 programs Windows runs emulated (ADR 018), and sqlite3 publishes no ARM64 Windows build and does not compile there.

**What.** Take native ARM64 builds of those tools when their projects publish them, and report or help fix sqlite3's build upstream.

**Done when.** The ARM64 Windows runtime carries no emulated tool, and `examples/my_cool_app` delivers on `windows-11-arm`.

### Linux system libraries a gem needs **1.0**

**Why.** A wrapper whose extension links a desktop library the target distribution provides, such as Tokra's WebKitGTK 4.1, GTK 3, libsoup 3, and JavaScriptCoreGTK 4.1, cannot ship through Fledge on Linux today: Fledge refuses a gem whose compiled files need a library that neither Linux's baseline (the manylinux_2_39 list), the runtime, nor the gem provides. Tauri's bundler lists such libraries as `.deb` dependencies and, for an AppImage, bundles them with linuxdeploy's GTK plugin. Tokra's session reported the need on 2026-11-03; a `fledge.yml` key that changes what a developer can ship is the agents' to design (AGENTS.md, Questions), and 1.0 freezes `fledge.yml`.

**What.** Design in an ADR, with prior art (auditwheel's policy and `--exclude`, Tauri's `.deb` `depends` and AppImage handling, linuxdeploy plugins, Debian Policy on `Depends`): a way for a project to declare the system libraries its gems may load, which the library check accepts for that project and the `.deb` lists as `Depends` (a soname to package mapping, or package names), and what an AppImage does with them (expect them on the host, or bundle them, with the cost of each). Keep it to the smallest key; refuse a declaration that names a library the gems do not need. Test with a stand-in gem linking a library that every CI image has.

**Done when.** A stand-in gem linking a declared system library builds on glibc Linux, the `.deb` lists its package as a dependency and installs with apt on a clean Debian and Ubuntu, and a gem needing an undeclared library is still refused with a message that names the key.

**Status.** Done (requested), ADR 030: `linux: system_libraries`, a list of sonames. The library check takes a gem that needs one and refuses one listed that no gem needs; a `.deb` depends on the packages dpkg-shlibdeps finds for them; an AppImage and an archive expect them of the host, and the Linux launcher names a missing one, with status 72, before Ruby starts. `examples/system_library` (a path gem linking libpng) built on `x86_64-linux` and `aarch64-linux`, its `.deb` depended on `libpng16-16t64 (>= 1.6.2)` and installed with apt in fresh `debian:trixie-slim` and `ubuntu:24.04` containers, its archive and AppImage named the missing libpng there first, and the project without its list was refused, naming the key (the `experiment/linux-system-libraries` branch, run 41962107216, every job green). Not yet: bundling the libraries into an AppImage, a Windows counterpart, and `.rpm`.

### Fledge on RubyGems.org, and runtimes that do not depend on one host **1.0**

**Why.** From 1.0.0 Fledge is also released on RubyGems.org, by a human; a git tag and a GitHub Release stay, and a release can be found either way (decisions, 2026-11-03, with the maintainer's explanation of 2026-11-04). Before 1.0 nothing is pushed there: the maintainer will not claim a name for a project that may fail. Fledge is one gem, pure Ruby, so there is one `gem push`. Its runtimes are Fledge's own builds (ADR 001, 003, 004, 011, 017), not upstream's files, and today every runtime and every LGPL source bundle that an app's `notices/NOTICES.txt` names lives only as a GitHub Release asset (ADR 004, section 5). A change in that host's terms would break both the download and the source offer of every app already shipped.

**What.** (1) Publish: a checklist for the one `gem push`, and the port of RatatuiRuby's version and git tooling, copied and not depended on (ADR 028, section 7): `rake bump:*`, `rake release`'s replacement of Bundler's normalized prerelease tag with the SemVer one, `release:update_stable`, and the lookup that finds the CI run for a tagged commit. The sources are `~/Developer/ratatui_ruby/tasks/bump.rake`, `tasks/release.rake`, `tasks/bump/*.rb`, and `tasks/release/*.rb` (at `10dd1800`), and the devtools gem's `lib/ratatui_ruby/devtools/tasks/` (at `1a31a719`). Copied code is the maintainer's own projects' and sits in SPDX snippets (AGPL-3.0-or-later, AGENTS.md, Licensing). `rake release` stays refusing to push until 1.0. (2) Make the runtimes' host replaceable: every runtime is already checked against the pins the gem carries (ADR 014), so any host serves it. Add an environment variable that names another place to download from, as `PYTHON_BUILD_MIRROR_URL` and `RUSTUP_DIST_SERVER` do for their tools (ADR 002, section 2), and a second default location, once the maintainer names a host. (3) Decide with the maintainer how the LGPL source offer outlives a host: ship the sources of libxcrypt, GMP, and libfuse with each runtime's notices, or point them at upstream's own archives by pinned hash (LGPL-2.1, section 6; `questions.md`).

Not taken: a platform gem per target that carries its runtime, as RatatuiRuby's and nokogiri's gems carry compiled code, which was built and proved on `experiment/platform-gems` (run 41959876834, all seven desktop platforms) and is kept as a later option under one gem name if offline installs matter. Assembling a runtime on a developer's computer from upstream's pinned Ruby: a Windows launcher built by a developer's rolling MSYS2 gcc cannot be pinned (ADR 001, section 8), Linux still needs the libxcrypt Fledge builds (ADR 004, section 4), musl and the phones have no upstream Ruby, and CI proves one pinned runtime (ADR 014).

**Done when.** The one gem builds from a release commit and installs and builds an app on every platform; the maintainer can publish it with a documented checklist; `fledge build` takes its runtimes from a place named by an environment variable, checked against the same pins; and the LGPL source offer does not name a host that is the only place it can be had.

### 1.0 **1.0**

**Why.** 1.0 tells developers the CLI, `fledge.yml`, and what a built app contains will not change without a major version.

**What.** When at least some of the unproven things are proven (a real iPhone or iPad and App Review, a real signing trust chain, Android on arm64), every other **1.0** story is done, the money-bound ones that are not proven are named as unproven in the README, and every **maybe 1.0** story is decided, review the CLI and `fledge.yml` for anything to break first, prepare `1.0.0` as a gem release for a human to publish on RubyGems.org (decisions, 2026-11-03 and 2026-11-04; the release is also found by its git tag and GitHub Release, which stay), and stop treating pre-1.0 breaking changes as free (AGENTS.md). Until then agents release by tag and GitHub Release, as ordinary 0.MINOR.PATCH versions, beginning with `v0.1.0` (decisions, 2026-11-02); the `0.0.1.pre.N` form ended at `v0.0.1.pre.17`. From `1.0.0`, releases follow ADR 028, section 7: a `release/1.0` branch cut from `trunk` with the ported `rake bump:*` tooling (the story above), backports by `git cherry-pick -x`, a SemVer tag on every release, `stable` at the newest, and the maintainer running `rake release`.

**Done when.** At least some of the three money-bound proofs are done, the gems of `1.0.0` are ready to publish and have passed an install-and-deliver check on every platform, and the maintainer has published them.

**Status.** The review of the CLI and `fledge.yml` is done (requested): `doc/contributors/research/2026-11-04-one-point-oh-surface.md`. Of its six must-change items, the contract is ADR 029; Ruby's version is ADR 028, on the maintainer's answer of 2026-11-03: Fledge has no default Ruby, and an app gets the Ruby that runs `fledge build`; the schema names its release's tag (ADR 023, section 3), and every release keeps its tag after 1.0 too (ADR 028, section 7).
