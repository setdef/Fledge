# ADR 062: Leave export compliance to the developer

## Context and Problem Statement

App Store Connect asks about an app's encryption on every upload unless its `Info.plist` holds `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption`. Writing `false` there would spare a developer the question. Every Fledge iOS app contains OpenSSL, which Ruby uses for HTTPS and the `openssl` library. Is `false` right for a Fledge app by default, and should Fledge's iOS template write the key?

Apple's text, read on 2026-11-07, does not make `false` clearly right:

- The key's value covers the app "including any third-party libraries you link against", and Apple asks for a determination when an app "uses, accesses, contains, implements, or incorporates encryption". A Fledge app contains OpenSSL, so even one that makes no connection does not plainly "use no encryption".
- The case Apple calls exempt by name is "encryption that's built into the operating system—for example, when your app makes HTTPS connections using `URLSession`". Ruby's HTTPS goes through the app's own OpenSSL, which that sentence does not name.
- Apple puts the claim on the developer: "you're responsible for all liabilities associated with misinterpretation of export regulations or claiming exemption inaccurately". The U.S. regulations it points to (EAR 740.17 and 742.15) could not be read from bis.gov's pages, so nothing rests on them.

## Decision Drivers

- Fledge makes no legal claim for a developer that Apple's text does not support.
- A developer who only runs an app on their own phone is not stopped.
- What Fledge leaves undecided, it says plainly, where developers read.

## Considered Options

1. Write no `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption`, report in CI's archive check whether it is declared, and say in `doc/phones.md` what Apple's pages say, as not legal advice.
2. Write `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` `false` in the template.
3. Refuse an iOS build whose `Info.plist` lacks the key.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen: option 1, because the answer is the developer's claim, and the developer's alone. Writing `false` would make that claim for every app, including one whose gems do more than HTTPS. Refusing a build would stop a developer who only wants to run on their own phone, for a key that only an upload asks about. App Store Connect's questionnaire is where a developer answers, once, and adds the key afterward.

Confidence: medium. It rests on Apple's pages as read on 2026-11-07, and not on the U.S. regulations themselves.

### Consequences

- Good, because Fledge claims nothing about any app's encryption.
- Good, because `doc/phones.md`, "Export compliance", quotes Apple's pages, marked as not legal advice, and says how to add the key.
- Bad, because every developer meets App Store Connect's questions on their first upload, and keeps the key in their own host's `Info.plist` (ADR 025).
- Neutral: a later reading of the regulations that made `false` clearly right for an app that only uses HTTPS would be a reason to write it in the template.

### Confirmation

`tasks/mobile_parity/ios_archive.rb` reports, for every archived app, whether `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` is declared, and `test/mobile_ios_archive_test.rb` tests that report. `doc/contributors/account-tests.md`, IOS-1 and IOS-3, are where a developer account tests an upload and a review.
