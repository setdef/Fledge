== Ship a Ruby App to People Who Don't Have Ruby

Fledge[https://github.com/setdef/Fledge] turns a Ruby program you already
run into an app that people without Ruby can download and run. Your users
unpack one file and run it. They install nothing, and never learn what Ruby
is.

  gem "fledge", git: "https://github.com/setdef/Fledge", tag: "v0.9.0", group: :development

=== A First App

A folder named +greeter+ holds a one-line +main.rb+ and a +Gemfile+ with
Fledge in it:

  $ bundle exec fledge build
  dist/Greeter-arm64-darwin.tar.gz
  $ tar -xzf dist/Greeter-arm64-darwin.tar.gz -C /tmp
  $ /tmp/Greeter-arm64-darwin/Greeter
  Hello, Ruby!

The archive is 20 MB. Anyone with an Apple silicon Mac on macOS 14 or newer
can run it, with no Ruby on their computer.

=== What You Get

Fledge copies your program, your project, and the gems your
+Gemfile.lock+ locks into a Ruby that runs from any folder, and adds a
launcher that starts your program as <tt>bundle exec</tt> would. Your code
runs as it does under <tt>ruby main.rb</tt>, whether or not your users
installed a Ruby.

[Terminal apps]
  A <tt>.tar.gz</tt> on macOS and Linux, a <tt>.zip</tt> on Windows, with a
  SHA-256 beside each for Scoop, Homebrew, and winget.
[Windowed apps]
  A <tt>.app</tt> and a <tt>.dmg</tt> on macOS, a setup program on Windows,
  and an AppImage, and a <tt>.deb</tt> if you ask, on Linux, with your name, icon,
  version, and the files your app opens.
[Phones]
  An Xcode or Gradle project for iPhone, iPad, and Android, with your C
  and C++ gems compiled in.
[Frameworks]
  <tt>fledge build --from</tt> makes a new app from one Fledge already
  built, with no Bundler, network, or compiler on the computer.

Fledge builds for macOS on Apple silicon, Linux (glibc and musl, x86-64 and
ARM64), and Windows (x64 and ARM64). Build each platform's app on a computer
of that platform, such as a CI runner of each.
