# ADR 040: Hide the person's Ruby settings from Ruby's boot, and restore them before the program runs

## Context and Problem Statement

Ruby, RubyGems, and Bundler read their settings from environment variables: `RUBYOPT` and `RUBYLIB` change what Ruby loads, `GEM_HOME` and `GEM_PATH` where RubyGems looks, `BUNDLE_GEMFILE` which bundle Bundler sets up, and `BUNDLER_SETUP` names a file `rubygems.rb` requires as it loads. A person who runs an app may have a Ruby of their own, with its settings in their environment.

Two rules pull apart. Nothing of a Ruby the person happens to have may change how the app runs (AGENTS.md, Design precedence 1). But the app's `ENV`, and every program it starts, should look as they would under `ruby main.rb` (decisions 9 and 10; Design precedence 2). Removing the variables for good meets the first and breaks the second. The orchestrating agent asked the maintainer whether to restore them after Ruby boots, and recommended it. He answered (`decisions.md`, 2026-10-26, later): "11. I think yes?"

## Decision Drivers

- The person's settings cannot change how the app boots or which gems it loads (Design precedence 1).
- The app's `ENV`, and the environment its child processes inherit, are what `ruby main.rb` or `bundle exec` would give (decisions 9 and 10).
- Nothing of the mechanism shows in `$LOADED_FEATURES`, `$LOAD_PATH`, or the app's globals (ADR 081).

## Considered Options

1. Remove the person's Ruby settings before Ruby starts, for good.
2. Hide them from Ruby's boot, and put them back before the app's program runs.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen: option 2, as the maintainer answered.

Before Ruby starts, the launcher sets aside and removes every variable whose name begins with `RUBY`, `GEM_`, `GEMRC`, `BUNDLE_`, or `BUNDLER_`, and RubyInstaller's `RI_FORCE_PATH_FOR_DLL`; on Windows, whose variable names ignore case, in any case. Prefixes, not a list of names, so a variable a later Ruby adds is hidden too. After `ruby_options` returns, when Ruby has booted, RubyGems has loaded, and the program is compiled but not run, the launcher has RubyGems fix its paths with `Gem.paths`, which reads `GEM_HOME`, `GEM_PATH`, and `GEM_SPEC_CACHE` once and keeps the answer; for an app with a Gemfile it has Bundler set up the app's gems (ADR 045); then it puts every variable back, with its own value, or for an app with a Gemfile beneath Bundler's own settings as `bundle exec` composes them; then the program runs. It does this in C, between `ruby_options` and `ruby_run_node`, so nothing joins `$LOADED_FEATURES`, as a `-r` boot file would. Ruby's own `gem_prelude.rb` sets the precedent: it removes `BUNDLER_SETUP` while RubyGems loads and puts it back afterwards. `doc/contributors/design/launcher.md`, "Hiding and restoring the person's Ruby settings", gives the order and the rules.

Confidence: high. The parity check runs every comparison with a person's `GEM_HOME`, a `RUBYOPT` of `-W0`, and two marker variables, and Delivery runs every app in a hostile environment.

### Consequences

- Good, because a hostile `RUBYOPT`, `RUBYLIB`, `GEM_HOME`, `GEMRC`, or `BUNDLER_SETUP` cannot change the app's boot or its gems.
- Good, because the app's `ENV` holds the person's settings, and a child Ruby the app starts (`system(RbConfig.ruby, ...)`) runs with them, as under `ruby main.rb`, and in an app with a Gemfile with Bundler's over them, as under `bundle exec`. An app that wants its children kept apart clears those variables itself.
- Bad, because two differences from `ruby main.rb` remain, which `Parity::JUSTIFIED` and `doc/differences_from_ruby.md` list: `$VERBOSE`, which a person's `RUBYOPT=-W0` makes `nil` under `ruby` and leaves `false` in the app, and `Gem.path` and `Gem.dir`, which never hold the person's `GEM_HOME` or `Gem.user_dir`.
- Bad, because a person cannot turn on, for an app, anything Ruby reads only at boot, such as `RUBY_YJIT_ENABLE` or `RUBY_MN_THREADS`.
- Neutral: what Ruby and RubyGems read after boot still reaches the app, as under `ruby main.rb`: `GEM_REQUIREMENT_<NAME>` when a gem is activated, `GEMRC` when RubyGems' configuration is first read, `BUNDLE_*` when an app without a Gemfile requires Bundler, and `RUBYSHELL` when an app starts a shell on Windows.
- Bad, because the list of prefixes is read against each Ruby's source (`runtime/launcher/scrub.c` says what each covers), and a new Ruby may read a variable outside them at boot.

### Confirmation

- The parity check (`tasks/parity.rb`) compares `ENV`, the `ENV` a child Ruby sees, `Gem.path`, `Gem.dir`, and `$VERBOSE` with the person's settings in place.
- `test/integration/built_app_test.rb` runs built apps in no Ruby environment, in a hostile one, and with a person's settings, and checks what their children see; `test/integration/launcher_environment_test.rb` starts the launcher with environments `execve(2)` passes as given.
- Delivery (`.github/scripts/deliver.sh`, `deliver.ps1`) runs every app in a hostile environment and asks the probe what a child Ruby sees, inside and outside `Bundler.with_original_env` and `with_unbundled_env`.

## More Information
